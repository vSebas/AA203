
\section*{2.3 Cart-Pole Balance}

\subsection*{2.3(a)}
Start with the Euler approximation
\[
    s_{k+1}\approx s_k+\Delta t f(s_k,u_k)
\]
Now linearize around $\bar s=(0,\pi,0,0), \bar u=0$, and use
\(\tilde s_k=s_k-\bar s\). This gives
\[
    \tilde s_{k+1}\approx
    \left(I+\Delta t\frac{\partial f}{\partial s}(\bar s,\bar u)\right)\tilde s_k
    +\Delta t\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(\bar s,\bar u)u_k
\]
So the discrete-time linearized system has
\[
    A=I+\Delta t\frac{\partial f}{\partial s}(\bar s,\bar u),
    \qquad
    B=\Delta t\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(\bar s,\bar u)
\]
Plugging in the Jacobians from the problem statement gives
\[
    \tilde s_{k+1}\approx A\tilde s_k+Bu_k
\]
where
\[
    A=I+\Delta t
    \begin{bmatrix}
    0&0&1&0\\
    0&0&0&1\\
    0&\frac{m_pg}{m_c}&0&0\\
    0&\frac{(m_c+m_p)g}{m_c\ell}&0&0
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    B=\Delta t
    \begin{bmatrix}
    0\\0\\ \frac{1}{m_c}\\ \frac{1}{m_c\ell}
    \end{bmatrix}
\]

\subsection*{2.3(d)}
\textbf{i.} We can reuse \(A\) and \(B\) because the dynamics Jacobians along
the reference do not depend on \(x\), and the desired trajectory keeps
\(\theta=\pi\), \(\dot\theta=0\). Then the same upright linearization is
valid along the reference.

\textbf{iii.} 
The pendulum must tilt to generate the horizontal cart acceleration from the reference, so keeping
\(\theta(t)\equiv\pi\) while moving the cart sinusoidally is not a dynamically
consistent upright trajectory without feedforward compensation.
